Forklift battery charging: a practical guide

Lead-acid forklift batteries give off hydrogen while they charge. Charge them only in a dedicated, well ventilated charging area, away from offices and break rooms, and keep the area clear of pallets and stored stock.

Post no-smoking signs at every entrance to the charging area and keep sparks, open flames and grinding work out of it. A single spark near a charging battery can ignite the hydrogen.

Anyone who checks or tops up battery water must wear chemical-resistant gloves and safety goggles, or a face shield. Battery acid burns skin and can blind in seconds.

Install an eye-wash station within ten seconds' walk of the chargers and test it every week. If acid reaches the eyes, rinse them for at least fifteen minutes and then get medical help.

Park the truck, lower the forks, set the brake and turn the key off before connecting the charger. Check the cable and plug for damage first, and never pull the plug while current flows.

Keep a log of each charge cycle, water top-up and fault. Report swollen, cracked or hot batteries to the maintenance lead and take the truck out of service until it has been checked.
